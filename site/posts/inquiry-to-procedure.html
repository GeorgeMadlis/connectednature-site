Sixth Inquiry

From Inquiry to Procedure: Human–AI Research as Progressive Reduction

How a recorded project-specific inquiry trajectory can be represented at the right granularity; how selected lessons can be converted into code, tests, specifications, instructions, and explicit uncertainty; and why the resulting operational method should normally work without replaying the whole history that helped produce it.

Originating statement

“In human–AI research, a recorded inquiry trajectory is primarily provenance of method formation. Its possible methodological value lies in whether the project-specific evolution of an inquiry provides information needed for reliable continuation, correction, interpretation, revision, or transfer that is not already supplied by participants' prior knowledge, shared explicit knowledge, and a sufficiently explicit current project state. Where that value exists, a disciplined method-evolution process should compile selected findings into a current operational method: code, tests, validators, specifications, AI instructions, evidence semantics, and explicit unresolved questions. Successful reductions should allow reduced lessons to leave active historical context where possible, while the archived history remains available for audit and later method revision.”

The statement is deliberately more careful than the earlier version of this article. It separates objects that are easy to collapse. The underlying trajectory is the historical movement of questions, understanding, claims, evidence, frames, procedures, and determinations. A recorded inquiry is one proportional representation of that movement: a source-grounded ordered record sufficient to reconstruct materially relevant changes without claiming formal OKF-FC bundle conformance. A lightweight formal bundle and a full formal bundle are stronger representations with additional machinery. A method-evolution protocol is the procedure that evaluates findings in the record and decides whether they should become operational artifacts or remain provenance only. The operational method is the present set of specifications, procedures, code, tests, validators, instructions, evidence semantics, and explicit unresolved questions needed to perform the research task now.

The meaningful choice is therefore not simply no record versus formal bundle. It is a representation choice: ordinary notes, recorded inquiry, lightweight formal bundle, or full formal bundle. The governing design principle is representation proportionality: preserve enough structure for the intended future use, and require additional formal machinery only when expected epistemic, audit, reproducibility, experimental, or repeated-use value justifies it. This article does not claim that a universal promotion threshold has been validated.

The crucial distinction is this: historical dependence of method discovery is not the same as operational dependence of method execution. A history may have contributed materially to discovering a procedure while no longer being needed to apply that procedure. Conversely, showing that a final procedure can run without its historical log does not prove that the history was useless in discovering it.

The central proposition is therefore deliberately falsifiable. A recorded project-specific inquiry trajectory has incremental value only when access to it enables materially better method formation, continuation, diagnosis, correction, transfer, or revision than can be achieved from observer background knowledge, shared explicit knowledge, and a sufficiently explicit current project state alone. If another capable human–AI team can reliably derive the same or a better method from the current state without explicit trajectory access, then the trajectory has not demonstrated an incremental operational advantage for that task.

The negative therefore has to be split. A formation negative would hold if an investigator without the project-specific trajectory could reliably derive the same or a better operational method from prior knowledge, shared explicit knowledge, and a well-described current state; in that case the trajectory would not have demonstrated incremental value for method formation beyond provenance and auditability. A revision negative would hold if archived provenance also provided no material advantage when diagnosing and repairing later method failures; in that case its durable role would be primarily provenance. The case-study evidence used below does not experimentally test either counterfactual. Nor would routine execution without history be a negative result. It is the expected shape of a successful reduction: current operational method plus new evidence should be enough to produce a result, with archived provenance reopened when revision becomes necessary.

“Trajectory-blind” must also be interpreted causally, not only at the moment a later agent is run. A prompt, test, validator, reading guide, source hierarchy, or agent instruction may already embody lessons compressed from earlier trajectory. An agent that never sees the historical rounds may still benefit from them indirectly if those lessons have become part of the current operational method.

This article does not start from a blank evidence architecture. The preceding Fifth Inquiry in this series, Evidence First, Consensus Later, examined the object- or world-facing level: how environmental observations, evidence, claims, disagreement, review, and governance can be represented without prematurely converting contestation into authority or consensus. This inquiry moves one level upstream. The evidence architecture that produces that representation is itself provisional and corrigible. Categories, bundle boundaries, validation rules, source roles, and agent instructions may initially be wrong, and making an incorrect architecture reproducible, deterministic, or inspectable does not make it correct. This article therefore asks how human–AI inquiry can discover, repair, and formalise that architecture as operational method.

Why this matters

Generative AI lets one person operate across a larger fraction of a technical project's lifecycle: requirements, data discovery, software development, tests, documentation, statistical interpretation, regulatory analysis, and domain-specific research. That breadth can be productive, but it does not create equal expertise at every layer. As one person covers more of the lifecycle, parts of the work are performed farther from that person's deepest competence, and the AI system's apparent fluency can hide the boundary between genuine method and plausible execution inside a weak frame.

No observer starts from zero. A human arrives with education, experience, disciplinary conventions, tacit knowledge, assumptions, and biases. An AI system arrives with a different and largely implicit body of learned regularities and capabilities, plus the explicit prompt, context, retrieved sources, tools, and instructions it is given. That background is real history, but it is not the same thing as the recorded trajectory of this particular project.

Three layers therefore need to be separated. Implicit accumulated history lives in the observer: human learning and judgment, or model training and capability. Explicit scholarly compression lives in transferable forms such as literature reviews, papers, textbooks, standards, specifications, documentation, code, and accepted methods. Local project trajectory records how this investigation changed: assumptions, frames, evidence, errors, corrections, supersessions, method changes, and unresolved questions. The first two are ordinary conditions of inquiry. The third is what this article is experimentally evaluating as an additional explicit research object.

Both human and AI inquiry are bounded, and they are bounded in ways that compound. Sensors and datasets capture only part of the world. Scientific knowledge is itself incomplete. A given researcher commands only a fraction of the relevant science: someone fluent in geospatial processing may be unfamiliar with a specific regulatory distinction, while someone who knows the law may misjudge remote-sensing uncertainty. An AI system works within the context, tools, and sources it is handed, and within the limits of its own reasoning.

Long-running projects are also not one continuous conversation. A task specification may be developed in one ChatGPT project, transferred by a human into Claude Code, materialised as a repository change, and later inherited by Codex or another model that sees the changed repository but not the earlier prompt-development process. The project is therefore a partially observed, multi-agent, multi-framework process. The recorded trajectory is only a projection of the materially relevant development process, not the whole process itself.

These bounds interact. A human supplies an initial decomposition that carries implicit assumptions, an imperfect breakdown, or ambiguous terminology. The model executes inside that frame and can amplify it rather than question it. The result is systematic scope drift that neither party introduced alone. If this is the common failure mode, then recording an inquiry has methodological value for formation or revision only when a process can use the record to expose and repair the framing — not when the record merely stores it. The distinction between a log that documents error and a log that helps correct it is the practical stake of this article.

The consequence for evidence-first governance is a constraint, not a proof. “Evidence first” does not mean that the evidence schema is given first. The architecture used to classify, bundle, validate, compare, and contest evidence is itself a provisional research output, and it can contain framing errors, scope drift, inappropriate bundle boundaries, mistaken source classifications, or invalid decision rules — an explicit evidence schema is not thereby a correct one. It must therefore be corrigible, versionable, and contestable in the same way the claims it structures are. The two case-study bundles examined here—the self-referential framework-self bundle and the applied eudr-coffee-brazil-fazenda-sucuri bundle—show that this kind of correction is not confined to fixing data: it includes identifying that a boundary, observer category, or claim class was incorrectly specified — as when the self-referential bundle's own object of study had to be widened at round four, demoting disagreement to one stage among five. Separately, the fabricated-input episode discussed later shows that reproducible, hash-verified provenance can still preserve an invalid evidence state when source authenticity has not been established. Neither was an error in applying a correct schema; each was an error in the schema itself. The implication is specific: before evidence infrastructure can support contestable governance, the machinery used to structure that evidence must itself be open to correction. This does not require that every such correction remain permanently active; the architecture remains provisional where it has not yet survived scrutiny, not indefinitely by default.

Research question

What additional value does an explicit inquiry trajectory provide beyond prior knowledge and a well-specified current state? Prior knowledge here includes both observer background and shared explicit knowledge, including literature, standards, documentation, code, and accepted methods.

Operationally: under what conditions does access to the recorded project-specific trajectory improve method formation, continuation, correction, revision, interpretation, or transfer beyond what another observer can achieve from their own background knowledge, shared explicit knowledge, and the current project state?

Five subsidiary questions sharpen it. First: how much useful historical learning is already carried by current code, tests, specifications, prompts, instructions, source semantics, reading guides, and other compiled artifacts? Second: does explicit trajectory access add further value beyond those compiled carriers? Third: when have historical lessons been reduced sufficiently that the trajectory can leave routine AI context while still remaining reopenable for verification and repair? Fourth: when does a later contradiction, source failure, frame change, regulation change, agent-configuration change, or new case make archived trajectory information valuable again? Fifth: when does trajectory information help different human–AI combinations compensate for differences in background knowledge and framing, and when does it instead create anchoring, stale assumptions, or context overload? Across those questions runs a further design problem: how much structure should be retained for different classes of inquiry, and when does expected future value justify promotion from a recorded inquiry to a lightweight or full formal bundle?

The answer must keep three claim levels separate. The descriptive claim is that recorded cases show observable changes in evidence models, assumptions, frames, agent configurations, operational constraints, and later artifacts. That claim is supported by the present record. The methodological hypothesis is that a disciplined process can sometimes use such records to derive, transfer, verify, or repair a better operational method. That is plausible and testable. The stronger causal hypothesis is that explicit trajectory access itself causes better method formation or revision than an equivalently rich current-state handoff whose compiled artifacts already contain prior learning. That stronger claim is not established here.

What exactly is being recorded?

The phrase “recorded trajectory” hides several layers that need to be kept distinct. The underlying trajectory is the movement of the inquiry itself. A recorded inquiry is a proportional representation of that movement below formal bundle conformance. A formal bundle, whether lightweight or full, is a stronger representation with explicit OKF-FC profile machinery. Git and OKF provide versioning and representation substrates, while the method-evolution protocol decides what later use should be made of the retained record. None of these is a transcript of cognition, and none is supplied by Google OKF alone.

Layer 1 — representation substrate. Open Knowledge Format supplies the portable representation substrate: human- and agent-readable Markdown concepts, YAML frontmatter, hierarchy, and links. This project’s OKF-FC and bundle conventions originated as a profile and extension of OKF v0.1. Upstream Google OKF is now v0.2, which makes provenance, trust, lifecycle, and attestation more explicit and first-class while remaining deliberately minimally opinionated. It also permits producer-specific extensions. OKF therefore structures a portable knowledge object, but it does not define this project’s theory of research evolution, nor does it define fc-round, fc-stage, fc-party, fc-supersedes, contestation stages, observer-disagreement semantics, or the project’s method-evolution protocol.

Here fc- is the project-specific namespace prefix for its fact-checking and statement-evolution metadata; these fields are project extensions, not fields defined by upstream OKF. fc-round records the bundle round, fc-stage the research-evolution stage, fc-party the contributing party, and fc-supersedes the earlier concept version replaced by the current one.

Layer 2 — version/provenance substrate. Git supplies version-addressable states, attribution, diffs, and history. Hashes and published snapshots make particular states inspectable and pin what later readers or agents are referring to. Put simply: OKF structures the knowledge object; Git records and version-addresses changes to that object. Neither alone explains the epistemic meaning of a change.

Layer 3 — research-evolution semantics. The observer-disagreement and OKF-FC framework adds research-specific semantics on top of OKF and Git. Existing formal bundle conventions include, where applicable, fc-round, fc-stage, fc-party, fc-supersedes, per-round inquiry records, bundle/repository ledgers, agent participation rules, explicit frame changes, and supersession. Recorded inquiries deliberately use less machinery: neutral metadata, ordered source-grounded records, and enough source-role and transition detail to reconstruct the materially relevant path. The current bundle-evolution analysis further proposes a richer relation vocabulary distinguishing at least supersedes, contradicts, reframes, reopens, and supersedes-under-assumptions. That typed relation set is a proposal from the current inquiry, not a fully implemented convention across all bundles. The distinction matters because a linear supersedes chain can hide whether a later state changed because the evidence changed, because assumptions or scope changed, or because the procedure itself changed.

Layer 4 — method-evolution protocol. The final layer is procedural rather than representational. The method-evolution protocol decides what to do with recorded findings: retain them as provenance, compile them into code, compile them into a test or validator, change a specification, change an AI instruction, retain explicit uncertainty, retain a conflict, reopen an earlier issue, or make some other justified operational revision. This is where the article’s distinction among recorded trajectory, method-evolution protocol, and operational method becomes operationally meaningful.

The materially relevant development process is richer than the recorded article, bundle, or Git history. Call that larger process H_full. It includes observable repository changes, but it may also include human reasoning, temporary agent context, prompt development in another environment, uncommitted experiments, and model-specific prior knowledge. The recorded trajectory is therefore a projection:

H_{traj} = π(H_full)

The projection symbol matters. It prevents the article from pretending that the public record is a complete cognitive history. The trajectory is useful only to the extent that it records enough externally visible state to reconstruct material transitions later.

A materially important transition can be represented as an observable event:

e_t = (R_t, O_t, A_t, I_t, Q_t, X_t, R_{t+1})

Here O_t is the participating human observer; A_t is the executing AI or agent configuration; I_t is the explicit instruction state; Q_t is the task or prompt specification supplied to the agent; X_t contains material external inputs, including outputs produced in another AI environment; and R_t and R_{t+1} are the before and after research states. This is causal provenance without chain-of-thought. For example, prompt development in one environment, human transfer, an agent invocation in another environment, and a repository change are epistemically different from an agent deriving the same requirement from the repository alone, even if the final diff is identical.

Within a given state, the technical determination is:

T_t = f(E_t, F_t, P_t)

Here T_t is the evidentiary or technical determination at research state t; E_t is the evidence and relevant evidence versions; F_t is the frame, assumptions, scope, and definitions; and P_t is the procedure, method, or instruction state used to produce the determination. To avoid a notation collision: P_t in this equation means the operational procedure/instruction state at time t, not the higher-level method-evolution protocol that decides how the record should change the operational method.

A downstream legal or operational disposition, where the project has authority and evidence to represent one, is separate:

D_t = g(T_t, L_t, J_t)

Here D_t is a downstream disposition rather than the technical result itself. L_t is the applicable legal or regulatory state, and J_t is the implementation context: actor role, supply-chain topology, traceability arrangements, jurisdictional infrastructure, and competent-authority context where relevant. Earlier drafts and compressed formulations write the determination as a single D_t = f(E_t, F_t, P_t), folding the technical determination and any downstream legal or operational disposition into one symbol; this expanded form separates them, so that a change in evidence, frame, or procedure (ΔT) is not confused with a change driven by the legal state or implementation context (ΔD).

For EUDR work, the legal state is itself structured:

L_t = {L_t^{EU}, L_t^{guidance}, L_t^{benchmark}, L_t^{MS}, L_t^{producer}}

The components denote, respectively, binding EU legal text, operative guidance or interpretation, country or region benchmarking, Member-State implementation or enforcement state, and relevant producer-country law. A technical geospatial result may remain unchanged while a downstream disposition changes because law, guidance, implementation context, or actor role changes. That separation prevents regulatory drift from being misdiagnosed as scientific disagreement.

The research state can then be written as:

R_t = {C_t, E_t, F_t, P_t, T_t, D_t, Δ_t}

In that candidate record, C_t is the claim, question, or problem definition; E_t is the referenced and versioned evidence; F_t is the relevant frame, assumptions, scope, and definitions; P_t is the procedure or instruction state; T_t is the technical determination; D_t is any downstream operational or legal disposition actually in scope; and Δ_t is the stated reason for transition. This is a candidate minimum reconstructible state from the current inquiry, not a validated universal standard.

Evidence should also carry declared roles rather than appearing merely as a list of files:

ρ(e) ∈ {screening, corroborating, commodity-attribution, legality, traceability, ground-validation, decision-support}

A source used for compliance screening is not automatically a source of legal authority, commodity attribution, traceability, or ground validation. Role declaration is part of the method because it prevents the existence of an evidence layer from being mistaken for decision sufficiency.

To keep the observer-background correction explicit, use S_t for the broader project or problem state, not P_t. Let i index an observer. For observer i, the starting knowledge available at handoff can be written as:

K_i^0 = B_i + L + S_0

Here B_i is observer i's background knowledge and prior learning, including tacit competence, assumptions, and biases; L is shared explicit prior knowledge, including literature, standards, documentation, source archives, code, and accepted methods; and S_0 is the current project or problem state at handoff. B_i is partly latent. The bundle is not expected to record it exhaustively; it can externalize only assumptions, frames, decisions, or consequences that become observable in the project.

The local project-specific trajectory, as recorded, is:

H_{traj}: S_0 → S_1 → ... → S_t

The comparison is therefore not history versus no history. It is:

B_i + L + S_t
versus
B_i + L + S_t + H_{traj}

In this notation, let j denote another, potentially different observer. The operational research question becomes: under what conditions does access to H_{traj} improve what observer j can achieve beyond B_j + L + S_t?

A history-blind participant is not literally history-blind; they are blind only to the project-specific trajectory.

The compiled product of such a trajectory is also observer-relative. A remote-sensing specialist, lawyer, software engineer, Claude configuration, Codex configuration, or GPT configuration may compress the same record differently because their background knowledge and failure sensitivities differ:

C_i(H_{traj}) ≠ C_j(H_{traj})

The target is therefore not minimum history. It is a compact operational method M_t paired with queryable verification provenance V_t. Routine execution should depend primarily on validated code, tests, specifications, evidence semantics, instructions, and explicit constraints, while V_t preserves enough rationale and source provenance for later investigators to ask why the method has its present form.

H_full →(π) H_{traj} →(C_{o,a,τ}) (M_t, V_t) →(E_new) T_new

When new evidence or a new case produces an unexplained contradiction, invalid output, undefined state, or structural mismatch, the archived trajectory can be reactivated:

(M_t, V_t, E*, H_{traj}) → R_{t+1} → (M_{t+1}, V_{t+1})

A current bundle state can therefore be read, in the deliberately cautious and non-real-time sense used here, as a twin-like representation of the inquiry at that point. It represents a research state: claim, evidence, frame, procedure, determination, and transition rationale. It is not a literal copy of cognition, and it is not a copy of the physical world. The analogy is structural. The Digital Twin Consortium's definition links digital twins to integrated data-driven representations with synchronization at a specified frequency and fidelity; no claim is made here that an OKF bundle satisfies that industrial definition, and no automatic real-time synchronization is assumed.

The bundle is also not a substitute for observer knowledge. Observer i interacts with the shared external representation using their own B_i and frame F_i; observer j may later approach the same representation with different B_j and F_j. In a compact form, a reconstructible state might be updated as:

R_{t+1} = g(R_t, E_{t+1}; B_i, F_i)

The point is not that the Digital Twin-like representation contains B_i. It does not. One possible value of recorded evolution is that some consequences of observer differences become inspectable in the external record instead of remaining entirely latent.

The versioned sequence connecting such states is closer to a digital-thread-like trajectory: it preserves how the representation changes across successive human–AI interactions. Human–AI inquiry is part of the update mechanism. It can discover new data, revise a method, expose a frame error, alter source semantics, or reopen a previous conclusion. The point is not to introduce Digital Twin theory as another foundation of this inquiry, but to name the representational pattern that motivated the question.

This also clarifies the relationship between the preceding inquiry and the present one. The world-facing evidence representation and the research-facing method representation are coupled: object-level evidence conflicts or validation failures can reopen method-level inquiry, while method-level corrections change how later object-level evidence is selected, processed, represented, and interpreted.

The objective is not to preserve every prompt, transcript, intermediate thought, or temporary artifact. It is to preserve enough state for the intended future use: reconstruction, audit, later source weighting, method revision, reproducibility, or experiment. A recorded inquiry may be enough. Promotion to a lightweight or full formal bundle is optional and conditional, not a reward for being interesting. OKF supplies the representation substrate; Git supplies versioned history; the project-specific recorded inquiry and bundle conventions supply research-evolution semantics at different granularities; and the method-evolution protocol supplies the rules for using that history. The historical record is provenance; the disciplined use of that record to reconstruct, compare, discriminate, supersede, retain conflict, or reopen is method.

Evidence base

The strongest evidence here is not a literature but two formal human–AI trajectory cases held under version control. They are published as two self-contained bundles committed into the COIN research folder — each a flattened export carrying its own round ledger, per-round records, and, in the first case, a metrics table and a retrospective classification. The first bundle describes the observer-disagreement framework itself: a general and partly self-referential case in which the inquiry examines its own recording, across twenty rounds contributed by two agents. The second is a public reading snapshot of an applied geospatial bundle for Fazenda Sucuri coffee screening under the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR), with generated PDF, JSON, GeoJSON, and report artefacts omitted from COIN. What matters in each is not the number of rounds but the observable transformations: historically recorded findings later represented as code, tests, specifications, validators, agent instructions, evidence semantics, or explicit uncertainty.

The evidence scope is uneven by design. Of the trajectories discussed here, two are committed as formal public bundles in COIN. The MHS/world-models episode is preserved separately as a supporting recorded inquiry, not as a third formal bundle. The remaining EUDR episodes are drawn from the versioned private-framework development record discussed in the underlying analysis. Those additional episodes clarify the method claim; they are not presented as equivalent public bundle evidence, and no public bundle URLs are inferred for them.

The self-referential bundle records provenance of method formation directly. At round four, the log states that a human instruction widened the object of study from observer disagreement to statement evolution, demoting disagreement to one stage of a five-stage trajectory; the change is written as a frame transition with content hashes and supersession links on every affected file. The human's own framing was not a fixed input but something the inquiry had to revise, and the revision is inspectable rather than merely narrated. Later rounds show findings routed into operational destinations: provisional conventions are made normative and then executable as a generator and validator, and — after a foundation audit — the specification is repaired so that every future bundle must disclose its use of Wolfram observer theory as a formal analogy, enforced by validator rejection conditions rather than left to good intentions.

The MHS recorded inquiry illustrates a different recording granularity: knowledge enrichment before focused fact-checking. On 27 August 2026 Anthropic announced a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard, describing it narrowly as a shared, model-agnostic specification and standardized driver through which AI agents can discover, read from, write to, and safely operate programmable physical devices. The source-backed sequence now preserved in COIN is: initial MHS inquiry, MHS/Digital Twin understanding, Free Energy Principle and active-inference enrichment, the stronger world-model claim becoming questionable, explicit fact-check, and consolidation. The first two questions were knowledge-enrichment questions. Only afterward did the stronger headline claim — represented by AI Secret's “Claude Just Killed The World Models” — become the explicit claim object under examination.

Its methodological value is small but precise. A final-state-only reconstruction could begin directly with “Did MHS make world models unnecessary?” and still perform a competent fact-check against the primary MHS material and the external headline. What it would omit is how the human first developed enough conceptual structure through MHS, Digital Twin, and FEP inquiry for the stronger claim to become questionable. The episode therefore cannot prove that recorded history improves research outcomes. It shows only why a recorded inquiry can be useful for studying fact-check formation: it preserves how a focused checking question emerged from earlier understanding work. The AI Secret headline is an external claim/source object, not a participating observer in the human–AI inquiry.

The source insertion also exposes the migration problem in miniature. Before the mhs-world-models-claude-qa attachment was committed into COIN, this public post interpreted an underlying Q/A trajectory that readers could not inspect in the public repositories. The chain is now saved Q/A, recorded inquiry, retrospective analysis, public article. That gives the article a primary COIN artifact to point back to, instead of asking the article to serve as evidence for its own MHS interpretation.

The EUDR bundle offers a public worked example of corrected decomposition inside an applied screening task. Its rounds show review findings becoming concrete repairs: a Sentinel-2 visual defect is fixed, AOI administrative labels and report-map behavior are corrected, the coffee temporal mask is repaired so current coffee preserves the baseline footprint while “new commodity since baseline” remains separate, and a recurring regional overview gap is pushed down into the renderer and publish contract. A further question exposes a level of reduction still available. The regulation defines a deforestation-free condition common across the relevant commodities in terms of whether goods were produced on land not subject to deforestation after 31 December 2020, and Article 3 prohibits placing such commodities on the Union market unless that condition is met. Read this way, the method decomposes more cleanly than a one-piece “coffee mask” allows:

The originating public claim also remains evidence rather than disposition. A Coffee Watch/AidEnvironment case profile described native-vegetation clearing at Fazenda Sucuri between August and December 2024 and reported that part of the affected area fell within an FAO forest definition. That publication is relevant to screening, but it does not by itself settle the exact production geometry, forest definition, commodity attribution, or relation between detected clearing and coffee production. The bundle therefore treats the publication as an input to evidence assessment, not as a legal conclusion.

  • Layer A — commodity-independent land evidence. Was the land forest? Did deforestation occur after the cutoff? Which datasets support or contradict this?
  • Layer B — production linkage. Is this the plot or establishment on which the relevant commodity associated with the product was actually produced? EUDR due diligence requires the geolocation of production, so an observation that deforestation occurred somewhere is not sufficient on its own.
  • Layer C — commodity-specific evidence. For coffee, source-specific evidence matters. Here FDP refers to the Forest Data Partnership coffee-probability evidence used by the bundle, while MapBiomas refers to the MapBiomas coffee land-use/classification evidence. FDP-only means that the relevant coffee condition is supported by FDP but not by MapBiomas; MapBiomas-only means the reverse; and both-source agreement means that both sources support it. These evidence states cannot be treated as having the same warrant merely because they all concern coffee.

This is a good case precisely because it refuses to turn a screening signal into more than the evidence supports. The recorded sequence lets one inspect how a source-specific coffee assumption was corrected, why visual and report defects mattered, and how those findings were converted into more durable operational constraints. The correction is visible in the bundle's own source-specific overlap results: FDP reports 0.27 hectares of post-2020 baseline-forest loss intersecting new coffee, while MapBiomas and the both-source agreement layer report 0.0 hectares for the same conversion intersection. The bundle keeps these as separate source-specific overlap results and preserves their disagreement, not as alternative names for one metric or as numbers to average away. Its current answer remains human_review_required and pinned-not-reproduced, and it states plainly that the result is a screening flag, not a legal non-compliance determination.

The same distinction appears in private-framework episodes used here only as method observations. In a Ghana coffee task, lowering the FDP coffee-probability threshold from 0.1 to 0.01 changed the technical screening result from insufficient_evidence to possible_relevant_deforestation. The point is not that coffee suddenly appeared in the physical world, but that a changed procedure P can move T = f(E,F,P) while much of the evidence E remains fixed. The mature reduction is not “use the lower threshold”; it is explicit parameter semantics, sensitivity analysis, validation requirements, and rules preventing a probability threshold from being treated as ground truth.

Another private-framework correction made explicit that accuracy is not inherited by intersection. For a candidate overlap such as forest in 2020, post-2020 disturbance, and commodity evidence, published component accuracies cannot simply be multiplied into an AOI-specific decision-grade accuracy. That inference would require assumptions about independent errors, compatible class definitions, common spatial support, comparable reference periods, representative local validation, and fixed operating thresholds. Until those assumptions are justified, the intersection remains screening evidence rather than a decision-grade area estimate.

The strongest private-framework correction concerned source authenticity. Earlier Brazil rasters were pinned and deterministically reproduced, but later investigation found that the pinned inputs were not authentic provider-derived acquisitions. Hashes reproduced the wrong inputs. The operational lesson is that V_t must include verification provenance capable of distinguishing provider event, acquired record, pinned artifact, computation, and interpretation. Reproducibility controls the pinned artifact and computation; it does not by itself prove source authenticity.

Transfer to wood added a further frame correction. A coffee-shaped method was not sufficient for Liberia wood evidence because a concession is not a production or harvesting block, a tree or log source, or a shipment source. The method had to separate deforestation from degradation, preserve production/source geometry status, and treat JRC TMF and RADD as evidence-producing source/process components rather than standalone bundle boundaries. The lesson is about transfer: a method may be reduced for one class of commodity cases while remaining structurally inadequate for another.

The Liberia Area K record also preserves disagreement among non-equivalent evidence processes rather than treating them as repeated estimates of one quantity:

Evidence process Reported area (ha)
JRC forest baseline 2020 261,004.86
Hansen/JRC post-2020 loss on baseline 10,914.48
Hansen 10% canopy-baseline post-2020 loss 11,216.61
JRC TMF deforestation, 2021–2025 5,386.32
JRC TMF degradation, 2021–2025 7,088.13
RADD confirmed-alert area 13,502.16
RADD low-confidence alert area 4,569.93

These figures differ because the processes differ in sensing mechanism, event definition, temporal logic, spatial processing, and upstream dependencies. The disagreement is part of the evidence state. It can motivate targeted confirmation, not an unjustified average. The concession-scale result remains a human-review screening state because it does not establish harvesting-block, tree/log, shipment, or chain-of-custody linkage.

This is where the inquiry takes up a requirement motivated by the earlier inquiry rather than invented here. Preserving the FDP, MapBiomas, and both-source layers as disagreement — instead of averaging them into one number — and refusing to let a screening flag stand in for a legal determination are examples of the documented, uncollapsed disagreement Evidence First, Consensus Later argues a conflict bundle should preserve. That inquiry supplies the epistemic pressure; this one examines how repeated research can turn such pressure into executable method — source separation, temporal-masking rules, validators, screening logic, human-review gates, and agent instructions that keep the layers distinct by construction rather than by reminder. That the resulting code runs deterministically does not, on its own, establish that the layers were drawn correctly; a deterministic procedure and a correct evidence schema are separate properties, and only the second is at stake when the frame itself is wrong.

The evidence therefore supports a narrow claim: certain findings recorded in history were later represented in operational artifacts. It does not establish the stronger counterfactual claim that the same artifacts could not have been derived from observer background, shared explicit knowledge, and a sufficiently explicit current state without the recorded trajectory.

Source evaluation

The two committed bundles are strong evidence for some claims and weak evidence for others, and the difference must be kept explicit. The logs record observable state transitions: supersessions, corrections, boundary changes, and the artefacts each round produced. They do not contain the entirety of the human's thought process or the model's internal reasoning. They are records of research-state evolution, not transcripts of cognition.

This limits what the evidence can support. It can support claims about how a method changed and what each change yielded. It cannot support a complete reconstruction of why either party believed what they believed at each step, nor can it prove that access to the trajectory was necessary to derive the current operational method. Any conclusion that leans on those stronger claims is reading more into the log than the log contains.

The method here is repository archaeology, not a randomized experiment. It can establish that corrections occurred, that later states differed materially from earlier states, and that the current method contains artifacts derived from those corrections. It cannot establish that the recorded trajectory caused the correction, that chronological succession is caused-by relation, or that unrecorded parts of H_full were irrelevant. The result is association plus reconstructible provenance, not proof of causal necessity.

The self-referential bundle is unusually candid about this boundary, which strengthens rather than weakens it as evidence. Its metrics table computes the transitions it can measure and marks a whole family of observer-distance measures as not measurable, giving the reason in each case: only one contestation round exists, no timestamped competing predictions were recorded, no independent paired labels over a shared item set were kept. It even excludes one round from its own measurement target to avoid measuring itself in a circle. A record that refuses the claims it cannot support is more trustworthy on the claims it does. The applied bundle shows the same discipline on a different axis: its current answer is marked as requiring human review and its provenance as pinned but not reproduced — the artefacts and hashes were checked, but no qualifying independent rerun was performed — so the reader is told exactly how far the evidence has been verified.

External EUDR literature strengthens the descriptive premise without settling the causal trajectory question. Work on GFC2020 presents a globally consistent 10 m forest-land-use layer aligned with the EUDR forest definition, while also reporting omission and commission error and treating the product as non-exclusive and non-legally binding. Agroforestry work warns that a forest/non-forest ontology can erase distinctions needed for coffee, cocoa, rubber, and other tree-crop systems. Earth-observation work supports EO as independent, verifiable, long-term evidence infrastructure. Compliance and legal scholarship further shows that actor role, producer-country law, EU guidance, benchmarking, Member-State implementation, traceability, smallholder land documentation, market segmentation, leakage, and trade diversion can all matter. This literature shows that materially consequential histories exist. It does not show that explicit access to a recorded human–AI trajectory improves later method formation beyond a current state that already embodies the relevant lessons.

The MHS recorded inquiry has a parallel source-evaluation lesson. A secondary newsletter headline was the eventual object of a focused check, not supporting evidence for the claim that MHS made world models unnecessary. The disciplined move is to classify the source role, compare the claim with the primary announcement, and only then decide whether deeper conceptual analysis is warranted.

Competing interpretations

Provenance, external memory, and compilation substrate should not be treated as mutually exclusive interpretations of the same object. They describe different functions at different levels. Provenance names what the recorded trajectory is: a historical record of how the present state arose. External memory names one way a later participant may use that record: consulting earlier states, assumptions, rejected alternatives, or unresolved questions. Input to compilation or revision names a further role: material consumed by a method-evolution process when deciding what belongs in the current operational method.

The methodological object is therefore not the mere existence of the history. A long log is not a method just because it can be reread. Nor is a deterministic pipeline validated merely because some past round produced it. The candidate meta-method is the transformation protocol: classify the finding, judge whether it generalises, choose its destination, test it before making it normative, archive what remains, and reopen the provenance when a later failure makes the origin of the method relevant again.

Conventional scholarship is a strong comparison class because it already relies on radical compression of research history:

collective research history
→ current state of knowledge
→ gap
→ method/result

A paper normally omits most failed hypotheses, abandoned analyses, intermediate misunderstandings, exploratory conversations, and local mistakes. That omission is not normally considered a methodological defect if the final evidence, method, assumptions, limitations, code, data, provenance where required, and current state of knowledge are adequately communicated. This is not proof that trajectories are useless. It sets the burden of proof: what does the explicit inquiry trajectory preserve that a sufficiently good paper, specification, source archive, codebase, test suite, provenance record, and unresolved-issues list do not?

The candidate answer is not "more history" in general, but epistemic transitions that are easy to erase through compression. In a transition such as A → not-A, knowing only not-A may not reveal why A originally appeared reasonable, what falsified it, which assumptions changed, whether the correction was local or general, or why another observer might reconstruct A. That remains a hypothesis to test, not a settled justification for keeping every historical detail active.

The MHS example adds a weaker but common pattern. Trajectory value need not require a full reversal from A to not-A. It may also arise when the verdict remains approximately stable while the rationale changes materially: A' → A''. A later observer can agree that the headline was overstated while rejecting part of the intermediate reasoning or deciding that source evaluation should have come first.

A further interpretation is observer-relative compression. A detail that is redundant to one observer may be the discriminating clue for another. The same H_{traj} can therefore yield different defensible compressions for different human disciplines or AI configurations. This makes compression itself an object of inquiry: the issue is not how much history can be deleted, but what minimum reconstructible state preserves enough rationale for later repair.

This resolves an ambiguity in the earlier draft. The question is not whether the trajectory is provenance or method. It is provenance. The question is whether a disciplined process can use that provenance to form or revise a better operational method than could be produced from prior knowledge and a rich current-state handoff alone.

Critical synthesis

This article is not testing whether accumulated history and prior knowledge matter to research. That is already built into human education, AI training, literature, methods, and scientific communication. The unresolved empirical question is narrower: whether local project-specific epistemic evolution deserves to become an additional explicit research object, and under what conditions it can remain archival provenance rather than active method.

The recorded trajectories support a narrower, more defensible version of the proposition than the earlier article implied. The two formal case-study bundles show that bundle history can document the formation and correction of a method, and that findings from that history can be represented later as code, tests, specifications, validators, instructions, evidence semantics, and explicit uncertainty. The MHS recorded inquiry contributes a smaller lesson about formation: a focused fact-checking question can emerge after conceptual enrichment without needing formal bundle machinery. The EUDR sequence contributes a substantive operational result: source separation, temporal-mask rules, report validation, human-review gates, and the Layer A / Layer B / Layer C decomposition.

observe inquiry transition → judge future reconstruction/audit needs → record minimum sufficient state → recorded inquiry
promote to lightweight/full bundle only when justified → method-evolution/revision → operational method
operational method + new evidence → result

This sequence is the central model. The recorded trajectory belongs to provenance of method formation, but its representation level is selected by intended future use. The method-evolution or revision step decides what can responsibly migrate into the operational method. Routine execution should normally run from the operational method and the current or new evidence, not from a replay of the whole inquiry. Archived provenance remains available when a contradiction, frame change, source-authenticity failure, unexplained constraint, or new class of case makes revision necessary.

H_full →(π) H_{traj} →(C_{o,a,τ}) (M_t, V_t) →(E_new) T_new

This compact chain clarifies the desired endpoint. Routine execution runs from the compiled operational method and verification provenance, not from permanent replay of the entire trajectory. The trajectory becomes operationally relevant again when a later failure, contradiction, source update, new observer, or new case makes the present method insufficiently explanatory.

A further reason for archival retention arises when a later inquiry materially contradicts, invalidates, or reframes an earlier conclusion. A chain may be reducible for routine execution while remaining non-disposable for audit. The reconstructible-state model above clarifies why: if one state accepted a claim under one configuration of evidence, frame, and procedure, while a later state rejected it under another, the final state alone may not reveal what drove the change. Preserving enough of the transition to reconstruct both states permits epistemic reconstructibility: a later participant can rerun the fact-check under the competing evidence, assumptions, and procedure, identify what drove the change, and seek evidence capable of discriminating between the interpretations without restoring the superseded conclusion to equal standing. This does not require every superseded artefact to remain in active context. It requires a recoverable record of materially contested transitions.

This gives bundle history a function the current head cannot supply. A conflict bundle can preserve unresolved disagreement among observers within a single research state; a versioned bundle history can preserve materially relevant disagreement between research states, provided the superseded states and the reasons for transition remain reconstructible. Without that condition the history is merely a sequence in which the latest state overwrites the epistemic meaning of the earlier one, and neither form then prevents a later consensus or deterministic implementation from erasing the arguments from which it emerged.

Retention alone does not convert bundle history into method. A bare sequence of superseded states is provenance — it records how the current state came about. What makes it methodological is a discipline governing what to do with a material contradiction between states: reconstruct the competing claims, isolate the differing evidence, assumptions, and procedure, seek evidence capable of discriminating between them, and then confirm supersession, retain the conflict, or reopen the earlier state. This requires that preserved history distinguish supersession from contradiction and reframing rather than collapse them into a single linear chain, as the proposed typed relation vocabulary is meant to do. A bare supersedes link silently encodes the latest state's frame as progress. The historical record is provenance; the disciplined use of that record to reconstruct, compare, discriminate, supersede, retain conflict, or reopen is method.

The same evidence does not show that recording the trajectory produces a better method than could be derived from observer background, shared explicit knowledge, and a rich current-state handoff. It also does not show that archived provenance will materially improve later diagnosis or repair. The existing evidence therefore does not discriminate between the formation proposition and its counterfactual negative. That is a limit, but it is a productive one: the negative becomes empirically testable instead of only rhetorically available.

The conclusion is therefore modest. The trajectory should be understood primarily as provenance of method formation. The candidate methodological contribution is the disciplined process that turns selected historical findings into a current operational method and reopens that history when revision becomes necessary.

Read alongside Evidence First, Consensus Later, the two cases point to two related but distinct levels rather than one. At the object level, the sequence is world or environmental claim → observations and evidence → inspectable evidence state → disagreement and contestation → review → governance decision or possible consensus. At the method level, the sequence is human–AI inquiry → detection of evidence, frame, schema, or procedure error → revised method or evidence architecture → tests, validators, specifications, instructions, and deterministic procedures where justified.

The two levels are feedback-coupled rather than merely sequential. Object-level conflict, new evidence, or validation failure can reopen method-level inquiry. Method-level correction then changes future object-level evidence construction, processing, and interpretation. That is why a deterministic pipeline is not enough: if the wrong frame has been made reproducible, reproducibility stabilizes the error. Evidence infrastructure constrains what governance can responsibly decide, while inquiry and correction constrain what the evidence infrastructure itself is permitted to assume. Object-level evidence may converge toward an independent reference where that is possible; contested evidence should remain structured rather than falsely collapsed; method-level trajectory concerns how the evidence architecture itself is corrected and transferred.

The architecture also keeps different forms of “settled” outcome apart. An empirical issue may be resolved when discriminating evidence or a defensible reference observation strongly settles it. A reasoning pattern may be methodologically reduced when repeated inquiry has been compiled into a sufficiently reliable operational procedure. A governance question may be institutionally or normatively settled when a decision is adopted despite residual value trade-offs or underdetermination. Method reduction, empirical convergence, and governance consensus are related, but they are not the same process. The EUDR bundle's own current position — human_review_required and pinned-not-reproduced — shows the sequence deliberately stalled at review rather than carried through to a decision.

Counterfactual tests

The COIN research folder defines future tests for the trajectory effect. They have not yet been run. A simple “history versus no history” design would be misleading because current prompts, tests, validators, reading guides, and repository conventions may already carry compressed historical learning. The cleaner design separates three conditions.

X_0 = {problem, sources, generic instructions}

X_1 = X_0 ∪ {S_t, Φ_t, U_t, M_t, V_t}

X_2 = X_1 ∪ H_{traj}

Here X_0 is the minimally specified condition; X_1 is the compiled-state handoff, including the current project state S_t, explicit assumptions and frame constraints Φ_t, unresolved issues U_t, compiled operational method M_t, and queryable verification provenance V_t; and X_2 adds explicit access to the recorded trajectory. The contrast X_0 → X_1 estimates the value of compiled historical learning. The contrast X_1 → X_2 estimates the remaining incremental value of explicit trajectory.

Conceptually, the question is whether Q_j(X_2) > Q_j(X_1) for observer j. Q may refer to continuation quality, error detection, correction, transfer, robustness to frame differences, diagnostic accuracy, cost, or, where a defensible G exists, movement toward an external reference. It is not proposed as one universal scalar metric.

  • Trajectory-aware versus trajectory-blind method formation. Give comparable investigators the same problem, shared explicit knowledge, and project state, while recognizing that each observer still has their own B_i. Compare X_1 and X_2 on the decomposition, procedures, validation rules, instructions, guardrails, and open questions they derive.
  • Current-method-only operational execution. Give an agent the current operational method plus new evidence, without historical rounds, and test whether the task can be executed correctly. A positive result would show successful compilation, not that history lacked discovery value.
  • Method revision with versus without archived provenance. Introduce a contradiction, frame failure, source-authenticity problem, unexplained rule, regulation change, changed agent configuration, or new class of case. Compare revision from X_1 with revision from X_2.
  • Cross-observer handoff. Suppose B_A != B_B. Can a sufficiently explicit current-state handoff allow observer B to continue observer A's work reliably without H_{traj}? Does adding H_{traj} materially improve continuation, detection of hidden frame differences, correction, avoidance of reconstructed superseded assumptions, or transfer across disciplinary backgrounds? Or is X_1 already sufficient?
  • Verification reopenability. Hold routine output quality constant while introducing a later discrepancy. Ask whether participants can identify whether it arose from ΔE, ΔF, ΔP, ΔL, ΔJ, ΔA, or ΔQ. This tests whether V_t and, where needed, H_{traj} preserve enough causal provenance to diagnose failure without rebuilding the entire project.

These tests address one validation target only: whether access to the recorded trajectory changes or improves method formation, continuation, transfer, or revision compared with a trajectory-blind condition. A history-blind participant is not literally history-blind; they are blind only to the project-specific trajectory. These tests do not by themselves establish observer generality. Multiple Claude, Codex, or GPT runs selected, framed, prompted, and evaluated by one human investigator can show within-investigator robustness, but they are not independent human-observer replication. A stronger design would use different human investigators, H_1, H_2, ..., H_n, ideally allowing them to generate or frame their own trajectories rather than merely execute prompts authored by the framework's originator. AI configuration should also be treated as an experimental factor: trajectory condition × AI configuration × human investigator.

A separate validation axis is external-reference correspondence. Where a defensible independent reference state exists, successive bundle determinations can be compared against it:

δ_t = d(T_t, G)

Here T_t is the technical determination at research state t, G is an independently obtained reference state or observation, and d is a task-appropriate distance or error measure. A transition for which δ_(t+1) < δ_t is evidence of movement toward the reference state, subject to the uncertainty of both the bundle determination and the reference. Real research need not improve monotonically: convergence, temporary regression, recovery, and divergence are all possible. In a geospatial case, the reference might be independently collected in-situ measurements or independently validated field observations, with quantities such as area error, classification accuracy, precision, recall, F1, or spatial overlap used where appropriate.

This distinction connects directly back to Evidence First, Consensus Later, and it keeps three axes separate. Trajectory value asks whether X_2 improves inquiry relative to X_1. Empirical correctness asks whether determinations converge toward G where an appropriate G exists. Observer consensus asks whether different observers converge toward each other. A trajectory may improve transfer without improving truth convergence. Observers may agree while remaining wrong relative to G. A project may converge toward G without needing its full historical trajectory. External reference convergence is available only for some classes of claims. For an empirical geospatial claim, such as whether land cover changed at a location during a specified period, an independent reference observation may sometimes exist. For normative, legal, governance, or value conflicts, there may be no equivalent observer-independent G. Consensus later therefore does not mean never converge. It means converge where discriminating evidence supports convergence, and preserve structured disagreement where it does not. One important failure mode is false consolidation: several observers may converge on the same determination while an independent reference indicates that the determination remains materially off. In notation, observers can converge on T* while d(T*, G) remains materially non-zero. Observer convergence is not the same as reference-state convergence.

Implementation path

The proposition implies an operational lifecycle. First, observe an inquiry transition. Second, judge the future reconstruction, audit, reproducibility, or experimental need. Third, record the minimum sufficient state. That may be an ordinary note, a recorded inquiry, a lightweight formal bundle, or a full formal bundle. Promotion from recorded inquiry to bundle is optional and conditional, not the default destination of every useful research trail.

Fourth, classify the finding: evidence, frame, assumption, procedure, source semantics, instruction, uncertainty, or source-authenticity concern. Fifth, decide whether it generalises, remains local, or is unsafe to generalise. Sixth, map it to an operational destination: code, test, validator, specification, AI instruction, evidence-semantic rule, explicit unresolved question, or provenance-only record.

Verify before making the reduction normative. Deterministic compilation does not establish epistemic correctness; the fabricated-input/source-authenticity lesson is exactly that reproducibility can preserve an invalid evidence state. Remove successfully compiled history from routine AI context where possible. Archive the trajectory as provenance. Reopen it when failures, frame changes, contradictions, source authenticity problems, or unexplained operational constraints justify method revision.

Frame and schema corrections deserve special care inside this lifecycle. They change what the system treats as a bundle, source, claim, observer, evidence layer, validation boundary, or screening category, rather than merely changing a value inside an existing category. Round four's widening of the self-referential bundle's object of study qualifies. The coffee temporal-mask repair qualifies only in this narrower sense: it changed the relation among baseline coffee, current coffee, and new coffee since the baseline. Such corrections should remain inspectable in the research trail when they materially explain why the current method looks as it does, even after their lessons have migrated into current operational artifacts.

A companion design principle follows for the AI's role. It should not be limited to solving the problem as posed. It should also test whether the posed decomposition is supported by the governing evidence, and whether a more general or more precise formulation is required. The geospatial repository already moves this way: its agent contract requires review of sibling history while prohibiting silent generalisation of dataset-specific thresholds, commodity-specific inference, area-of-interest assumptions, source hierarchies, legal interpretation, or evidence semantics.

A useful operational classification is:

Historical finding Preferred destination Routine need for explicit trajectory
Repeated software defect Test or validator in M_t Low after validation
Stable procedural rule Specification or code in M_t Low
Agent failure mode Agent instruction plus configuration note Low to moderate
Source limitation Data-source registry and V_t rationale Moderate after source change
Frame or schema correction Specification plus retained rationale in V_t Moderate/high during transfer
Material contradiction Reconstructible conflicting states High during revision
Unresolved evidence conflict Explicit conflict state or human-review gate High until resolved
One-off cosmetic change Archival provenance only Very low

Data resolution, temporal frequency, and cost are proposed extensions rather than present results. A simplified objective can be written as J = Q(r,τ,Ω,m) − λC(r,τ,Ω,m), where Q denotes task-specific evidence quality, C total operational cost, r spatial resolution, τ temporal sampling, Ω geographic area, and m method. This un-subscripted J is a proposed cost-performance objective; it is distinct from J_t, the implementation context in D_t = g(T_t,L_t,J_t). The trajectory should record not only which method was selected, but why a higher- or lower-cost branch was rejected, so that a robust cost-performance rule can later be reduced into M_t.

Regulatory evolution is also a method input. A change in technical state can be written as ΔT = h(ΔE,ΔF,ΔP); a change in downstream disposition can be written as ΔD = g_Δ(ΔT,ΔL,ΔJ). Preserving that distinction prevents a result difference caused by guidance, benchmarking, Member-State implementation, producer-country law, actor role, supply-chain topology, or traceability context from being misread as a change in ecological evidence.

Limits

This article does not measure whether access to recorded project-specific trajectory improves method formation over trajectory-blind derivation from prior knowledge and a rich current-state handoff, whether archived provenance improves later method revision, how many historical rounds an AI still needs once good instructions exist, whether instructions derived from one trajectory transfer to another, or whether the observed pattern generalises across domains. It rests on two cases and cannot generalise from them to a law of method.

The cases also do not establish generality across independent human observers, convergence toward an independently measured reference state, monotonic improvement, or the proposition that consensus among observers implies factual correctness. Multiple agent runs under one human's framing would remain weaker than independent human-observer replication, and a shared determination would still need comparison with an independent reference observation where such a reference exists. A reference state is not metaphysically infallible either: field and validation observations can carry positional, temporal, measurement, sampling, and classification uncertainty.

Observer background is a central limitation, not a nuisance detail. B_i is partly latent and not exhaustively measurable. Human expertise and AI learned priors cannot simply be held equal. Comparing H+ and H− across people introduces observer-background variation; using the same human twice introduces carryover. A well-written current-state document, specification, codebase, test suite, provenance record, and unresolved-issues list may eliminate much of the apparent value of loading the trajectory. Conversely, a trajectory can introduce stale assumptions, anchoring, context overload, or authority bias. The current two case-study bundles still do not prove incremental trajectory value.

The recorded repository history is also only H_{traj} = π(H_full). The fraction of materially relevant history captured by π is unknown. The three-condition experiment can leak information: X_1 is intended to encode compiled historical learning, but a genuinely minimal X_0 must not smuggle in trajectory-derived prompts, tests, or reading guides. The inquiry also lacks systematic cost measurements for alternative spatial resolutions, temporal frequencies, and confirmation strategies; it lacks a representative dataset of practical EUDR outcomes from operators, traders, authorities, customs processes, or alternative market channels; and independently defensible reference states G exist only for some bounded empirical subproblems.

The central hypothesis should be weakened or rejected if compiled-state handoffs X_1 consistently perform as well as or better than explicit-trajectory handoffs X_2; if apparent trajectory benefits disappear after controlling for information already encoded in prompts, instructions, tests, validators, and code; if trajectory-aware teams show more anchoring or slower correction; if archived history adds substantial maintenance cost without measurable repair, verification, or transfer benefit; if the useful information can always be encoded into a compact current rationale and verification record without retaining trajectory structure; or if differences attributed to trajectory access disappear under independent human replication or reverse unpredictably across AI configurations.

It also keeps a deliberate distance from Wolfram's vocabulary. Terms such as computational irreducibility and the bounded observer are useful for naming why a process may resist shortcutting and why a partial observer sees a coarse-grained world. They are used here as vocabulary, not as a demonstrated explanation. In particular, the absence of a discovered deterministic reduction is not evidence of genuine irreducibility; it may only mean the reduction has not been found yet. Deterministic execution and epistemic correctness are separate properties and are treated as such throughout.

Open questions

What additional value does project-specific trajectory provide beyond B_i + L + current state?

When is a well-specified current state sufficient for continuation, execution, or method revision?

When does trajectory compensate for observer-background differences, and when does it add noise?

Which epistemic transitions require reconstruction rather than summary?

When does loading history hurt by introducing stale assumptions, anchoring, context overload, or authority bias?

Once the operational method exists, which historical materials can leave active AI context without lowering execution quality?

When a later inquiry materially contradicts, invalidates, or reframes an earlier conclusion, how should the candidate minimum reconstructible state be tested against real evidence-driven, procedure-driven, and frame-driven change without preserving the entire trajectory?

Can the minimum reconstructible state preserve the needed transition without retaining the whole trajectory?

Does trajectory-aware handoff improve convergence toward G where G exists, and which evidence, frame, or procedure changes produce improvement, regression, or recovery?

How should the framework distinguish empirical convergence toward a reference state from institutional or normative consolidation where no equivalent observer-independent reference exists?

What cost-performance frontier governs spatial resolution, temporal sampling, sensor choice, local validation, and review effort, and when should a more expensive evidence path be justified?

When a result changes, how can the framework distinguish scientific or evidentiary disagreement from regulatory drift, implementation-context change, or changed actor role?

Which trajectory-derived lessons transfer across domains, and which are artifacts of EUDR, geospatial evidence, one investigator's framing, or a particular AI configuration?

When is a compact rationale plus queryable V_t sufficient, and when is deeper access to H_{traj} needed to prevent an apparently efficient compression from becoming opaque?
