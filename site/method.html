Method

How a claim becomes a researched article

ConnectedNature uses a simple but disciplined workflow: frame the statement, gather evidence, evaluate the sources, test competing interpretations, and write a synthesis that stays answerable to its limits.

The approach is partly inspired by Andrej Karpathy's idea: "feed raw data into an LLM to compile a structured, self-writing knowledge base." ConnectedNature applies that idea to selected statements and questions while keeping human judgement, source evaluation, and public readability central.

From claim to article

  1. Originating statement or question. The inquiry begins with something specific enough to test, not a vague topic area.
  2. Evidence gathering. Relevant material is collected across primary sources, scholarly work, historical case studies, ecological research, and where needed technical or institutional documents.
  3. Source evaluation. Each source is read for method, scope, reliability, institutional context, and likely blind spots.
  4. Competing interpretations. The page identifies more than one serious explanation whenever the evidence allows it.
  5. Critical synthesis. The article states what the evidence supports, what it only suggests, and what remains unresolved.

What counts as evidence

Evidence can include direct historical records, ecological observations, peer-reviewed scholarship, comparative datasets, government inquiries, archival material, and carefully bounded theoretical work. What matters is not prestige alone, but relevance to the claim under examination.

Why source evaluation matters

Sources do not arrive with equal weight. A vivid case study may illuminate a mechanism while remaining unrepresentative. A sweeping theory may offer explanatory power while masking uncertainty. Evaluation matters because public writing often inherits claims from sources it never properly tests.

Why synthesis is different from opinion

Opinion begins from preference and arranges material around it. Synthesis begins from the evidence trail and tries to describe what the record can bear. It still involves judgement, but that judgement is constrained by sources, counterarguments, and explicit limits.

How the research trail connects to GitHub

A ConnectedNature article is public prose. Its research trail is a working record. GitHub is used to make that record visible through versioned notes, source registers, question histories, and revisions to the article itself.

This matters for provenance. A reader should be able to see not only the published synthesis, but the path by which a claim was narrowed, challenged, revised, or left open.
